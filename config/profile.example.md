# Who the repo is judged for

This is a template. Copy it to `config/profile.local.md` (git-ignored) and describe yourself: repo2demo judges every
repo against that file. The more concrete it is, the sharper the verdicts.

## Role
- What you do, and for whom (yourself, a team you lead, an organisation, clients).
- The kinds of things you build: agents and automations, internal tools, web apps, data pipelines, dashboards, content.
- How you show your work: slide decks, before/after showcases, demo videos, workshops.
- So a repo can help in four ways: you building, your team building, the people who use what you build, or you
  presenting it.

## Where a solution may run (data sensitivity decides)
- **Sensitive data** (customers, staff, finance, anything internal): only approved platforms and formats, e.g. your
  existing enterprise stack, on-premises or local tools, models that keep data inside. No outside APIs or SaaS.
- **Not sensitive** (public info, demos, learning material, prototypes, presentations): any platform that does the
  job best.
- Ask for both: which of the two a repo fits, and what would have to change to use it with sensitive data.

## What you work with today
- AI and agents: e.g. Claude Code (skills, subagents, hooks, plugins, MCP servers), agent frameworks, automations.
- Apps and web: e.g. web apps, low-code tools, static sites, languages and RTL needs.
- Presenting: e.g. HTML slide decks, diagrams, motion and video as code.
- Data: e.g. your BI tool, warehouse, SQL/ETL.

## Constraints
- Machines: e.g. locked-down work PCs, installs need IT, browser policies.
- Licenses: e.g. no paid-for-organisations licenses without approval; prefer MIT/Apache/BSD.
- Home/personal projects may have none of these limits.

## What "useful" means to you
- Saves real time, adds a capability you do not have, or becomes a repeatable pattern.
- Makes your work easier to show (decks, visuals, before/after, demos).
- Clear path to real use, or a clear reason it stays a demo.
- Works on your OS. Easy to try in under 30 minutes.
- Small, maintained, permissively licensed tools over big frameworks.
